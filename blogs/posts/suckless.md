## Intro

  

If you've been using Linux for long enough, you may have heard about [suckless](https://suckless.org/). They are a software organization that claims to stand for and develop minimalist software for advanced users. However, I believe their philosophy has multiple flaws and is potentially toxic and hostile. Their development team has also espoused numerous concerning behaviors with regards to political affiliation.

  

A quick note that this blog will not be critiquing the quality of suckless projects themselves. This is primarily a critique of how their philosophies and views represent a wider problem of gatekeeping, toxicity, elitism, and political apathy in the FOSS community. Suckless' case is not unique; other organizations like [cat-v](https://harmful.cat-v.org/) espouse similarly juvenile, toxic, elitist beliefs (and in the case of cat-v, are embarrassingly openly libertarian). Other projects that have caught my ire in this way are [XLibre](https://github.com/X11Libre/xserver) and [Hyprland](https://hypr.land/). These projects have faced their own controversies for being headed by reactionary and sometimes downright hateful and unkind folks. In the future, I plan to write in a broad sense about how and why free software has failed to advance society in a meaningful way, but think of this as a "tip of the iceberg" example of that topic.

  

## Suckless Philosophy

  

[If you'd like to read their philosophy page, go here.](https://suckless.org/philosophy/)

  

"We strive to maintain minimalism and clarity to drive development to completion."

  

Minimalism isn't a bad idea in theory. However, the way suckless perceives it is flawed in my opinion. They treat as some holy abstract goal to achieve, instead of describing it as a clear quality present in any piece of software. It's a, perhaps purposefully, esoteric view on what "minimalism" is

  

"Many (open source) hackers are proud if they achieve large amounts of code, because they believe the more lines of code they've written, the more progress they have made. The more progress they have made, the more skilled they are. This is simply a delusion."

  

Lines of code does not have any direct correlation to code quality. To go as far as to say one is delusional for even thinking that lines of code points to some level of progress is, in my eyes, needless toxicity and elitism. This sort of behavior is what gets the

  

"Most hackers actually don't care much about code quality."

  

Who is most referring to here? This is another instance of suckless' blatant elitism and toxicity. They really claim only a holy subset of developers actually do anything to maintain code quality. I believe this also overlooks factors relating to code quality. Programmers are humans; they have emotions and thoughts and things they need to do to live. Many people program simply to keep the lights on, and simply pinning the blame on them or their supervisor glosses over the greater issue of software teams focusing on pushing out "good enough" services quickly due to managerial forces out of their control.

  

"As the number of lines of code in your software shrinks, the more skilled you have become and the less your software sucks."

  

More blatant elitism by pointing to arbitrary metrics.

  

Overall, I find their philosophy to be very hostile. One could be nice and just say it's "unapologetic" and/or that "advanced users will agree or be complacent with this rhetoric." However, I disagree. I've been operating with Linux and other Unix-like systems for several years, and I find these views to be toxic and abrasive, no matter the skill level of the user. By making arbitrary claims that "less code = good" and "no one cares about code quality", they delegitimize themselves as a serious software organization and come off more so as a bunch of edgy hackers with a superiority complex. This is specifically the type of behavior that gets people to turn away from the Unix-like community, and we should be waging the fight against proprietary software instead of maintaining toxic, elitist, and juvenile tendencies.

## What about those political views?

  

[A good resource on the topic.](https://tilde.team/~ben/suckmore/)

  

Suckless developers have, time and time again, exhibited behavior and explained views that are linked to many far-right philosophies, like Fascism and Nazism. A few examples linked above include:

  

- A [tiki march](https://www.cnn.com/2017/08/12/us/white-nationalists-tiki-torch-march-trnd/), associated with many far-right groups

- A developer calling their machine "Wolf's Lair" in German, the name of Hitler's military HQ

- Complaining about [Cultural Marxism,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_Marxism_conspiracy_theory) a common right-wing dogwhistle for Judaism and "woke culture" in general

- Spreading Nazi propaganda, like the [Dresden Bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Dresden) myths

- Alleged affiliation with Unite The Right (a white supremacist organizing event), though I have not been able to prove this

- Usage of Nazi symbolism (however links to proof are mostly dead)

  

This is all worrying. Far-right ideology is on the rise globally, and the longer we platform it and let it ride, the more it will fester. Projects like suckless should be called out on these behaviors wherever applicable. The truth that many "politically apathetic" free software enthusiasts fail to recognize is that there is no free software if the government outside your door is stripping rights away from you and your neighbors.

  

## So, what now?

  

If you would still like to use suckless projects after reading this, that is your directive. I am currently using [chadwm](https://github.com/siduck/chadwm) on my laptop, but I am looking into alternatives. Some good projects have spawned from suckless' work, like [awesome.](https://github.com/awesomeWM/awesome) However, between the concerning political affiliations and toxic philosophy, I find it difficult to rationalize supporting their organization.
